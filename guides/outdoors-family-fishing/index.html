Outdoors around Kojohama

Sea glass, forest trails and seasonal fishing, close to the cabins.

Kojohama is more than a seaside address. Within a short drive you can comb a beach for sea glass, walk quiet forest trails, take children bug-hunting in summer and, with the right rules in mind, try the fishing that Shiraoi anglers wait for all year.

Forest walks and hiking

Poroto Forest (Poroto Natural Recreation Forest)

A gentle choice for forest bathing and an easy walk, right next to Upopoy. Along the trails you can spot rare plants, wild birds and forest insects.

Mt. Tarumae

For guests who want a bigger day and a spectacular view: a longer drive inland. It is an active volcano, so check current trail and access information and weather before you set out, and choose it only if you are comfortable with a real hike.

With children

Ocean Stay’s coast and the forests just inland make easy, free playgrounds for children.

Beachcombing and tide pools at Ayoro Coast

A short distance from the cabins, Ayoro Coast mixes sandy beach with rocky shore. Hunt for colourful, wave-tumbled sea glass, unusual shells and driftwood, then look into the tide pools at low tide for small crabs, hermit crabs and little fish. The shells make good souvenirs. Watch the waves, keep children within arm’s reach of an adult and away from the water’s edge, and put living creatures back where you found them. You can drive, bike or walk to the beach, and from there an easy hike leads to the Ayoro lighthouse (the lighthouse itself is closed to visitors).

Hagi-no-Sato Nature Park (Ishiyama, Shiraoi)

A park of natural woodland with well-kept walking paths. In summer it is home to many insects, including stag beetles, grasshoppers and dragonflies, so bring a net and a bug box.

Poroto Forest

Beside Upopoy, with trails where families can look for acorns and colourful fallen leaves to use in nature crafts, as well as birds and forest insects.

Insects are a summer activity. Check any posted park rules, and take only what is allowed.

Seasonal fishing guide

Please read first. Fishing is strictly prohibited in Shiraoi Port. Strict protection rules also apply around river mouths, where salmon and trout run, so stay away from rivers and river mouths unless you have confirmed that it is permitted. Rules for salmon and trout are strict and change, so confirm current regulations, permits and local notices with the town or a local tackle shop before you fish, and obey any sign you see. Cold water, waves, wind and winter darkness are real hazards: never fish alone at night or in rough weather.

What local anglers target through the year, from the Shiraoi surf and the rocky shore near Kojohama. Catches are never guaranteed.

Spring (March–May): cherry salmon and flounder

As winter ends, anglers line up along the beach with lures for cherry salmon (sakuramasu) and white-spotted char (amemasu). The sea-run cherry salmon is fatty and pulls hard, which makes it very popular. Flounder such as magarei can also be caught by bait casting.

Summer (June–August): barfin flounder

As the water warms, fishing is best in the cooler morning and evening hours. The main target is the barfin flounder (matsukawa karei), a prized “phantom fish” that local release programmes now make possible to catch from the Shiraoi surf. It pulls strongly and makes exquisite sashimi. Fat greenling (aburako) can be targeted around rocky areas.

Autumn (September–November): salmon season

The busiest time of year, as chum salmon (akiaji) return to spawn. The most common method is Hokkaido’s distinctive float-and-lure rig, tipped with bait such as saury or bonito. Late in autumn, large sculpin (kajika) also come closer to shore.

Winter (December–February): sculpin and night fishing

Winter fishing involves cold water, ice, wind and short daylight hours. Sculpin is a seasonal target, but catches are not guaranteed. This guide does not establish permission to enter or fish in Kojohama fishing port. Use only locations where the current rules and signs explicitly allow fishing, avoid rough weather, and do not fish alone at night.

Before planning Mt. Tarumae, check the official road and trail notices. Closures can prevent access to the usual trailhead; do not assume the mountain or parking area is accessible.
