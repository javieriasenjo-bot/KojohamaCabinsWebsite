Travel guide

One slow day on the Kojohama coast.

A day in Kojohama is not remembered for how many places you saw. It is the colour of the sea in the morning, tarako for lunch, the warmth of an onsen, and the walk back to the cabin as the light goes. Here is an unhurried day built around real distances and real places. A car helps, but most of it can be done on foot.

The day at a glance

Times are a suggestion, not a schedule. Free up the whole afternoon and there is time for Upopoy (about 25 minutes by car) or Noboribetsu Onsen (14–20 minutes) — see “If the day feels long” below.

Morning — starting with the sea

No reason to rush

Every cabin has a kitchen, so breakfast can be made from whatever you picked up the day before. Coffee at the window, watching the Pacific, is genuinely the best part of this plan. Checking out today? Everything just needs to be ready before 10 AM.

Toward the Ayoro coast and the lighthouse

Walking straight from the cabin is the simplest morning route. Kojohama Station is only about 650–700 metres away, a 13-minute walk, so a loop that takes in the station area works well. On a calmer day, continue a little further toward the lighthouse.

The Pacific is stronger than it looks

Even on a still, clear morning, the waves here can be powerful. Stay back from breakwaters and rocks and follow the signs posted on site; in winter, check the wind and ice before you go far.

Lunch — what to eat in Kojohama

Kojohama’s food story begins with the Pacific. Tarako, salted cod roe, is the best-known specialty, followed by seasonal seafood and trout raised on local spring water.

Tacchan Shokudo

A local restaurant close to the cabins, known for its seafood donburi.

Tarakoya Kojohama

A long-established local stop selling tarako and other seafood, with dining as well. Good for buying tarako to eat over rice back at the cabin that evening, and it travels well as a gift.

Local trout

Nearby trout farms use clear spring water connected to Lake Kuttara. What is caught can be prepared as sashimi, fried, or salt-grilled.

These are small businesses. Closing on a particular weekday, finishing lunch service early or selling out are all normal. Check the day’s hours before setting out, and keep one alternative in mind.

Groceries — if dinner is at the cabin

Seicomart Shiraoi Kojohama

The convenience store near the station — drinks, snacks and the basics. It is a Hokkaido-only chain, worth a look even just for that.

Co-op Sapporo Noboribetsu East

For a proper shop, this is the option: a large supermarket about 3.3 km from Kojohama Station, enough for a full dinner’s worth of ingredients.

Buy it, then cook it

Every cabin has a kitchen and its own parking space right beside it, so buying and cooking is a natural rhythm here. Eating the same day’s seafood the same evening is one of the real advantages of staying in Kojohama rather than passing through.

Afternoon — warming up in an onsen

Kojohama is a quiet onsen area in its own right. After a coastal walk, especially from October through March, nothing suits the afternoon better than a hot bath.

Ayoro Onsen

A local, source-fed public bath known for sodium-chloride water — the simplest option after a walk.

Kojohama Onsen Hotel

A larger day-use bathing option. Published hours and prices can change, so confirm before you leave the cabin.

Hamachidori’s private family bath

Private-room bathing, useful for families or anyone who prefers more privacy. Confirm availability and session length directly.

New to Japanese onsen? Wash and rinse before entering the shared bath, and keep your towel out of the water. Tattoo, child and private-bath rules vary by facility — ask on site if you are unsure. Afterwards, hydrate, and do not drive if you feel light-headed.

Worth knowing: Yu Gelato operates inside the grounds of the 24-hour Hana no Yu Onsen, so a soak can end with handmade gelato in rotating local flavours.

If the day feels long

This plan leaves space on purpose. If the afternoon is still wide open, there is somewhere nearby worth the drive without changing where you are staying.

Upopoy — about 25 minutes by car

The national Ainu museum and park. It deserves half a day or more, so it is usually better as its own day than squeezed into this one. Reachable by train too.

Noboribetsu Onsen — 14–20 minutes by car

Jigokudani’s volcanic scenery and the hot-spring town. A half-day is enough to see it and still be back on the coast by evening.

Or go nowhere at all

Also a good choice. The point of this day is not the number of stops — it is staying in Kojohama.

How the season changes things

October–March

The coast empties out. The sea turns grey-blue, the wind picks up, and it is possible to walk ten minutes without seeing anyone. It is also the best season for onsen, and more time gets spent looking at the sea through glass than standing beside it.

Winter driving

Winter tires from the start of the cold season; four-wheel drive usually is not necessary. Main roads are cleared frequently and cabin parking is cleared as needed, but allow extra time after snowfall.

Warmer months

Longer daylight means room for both a morning and an evening walk without crowding the day.
