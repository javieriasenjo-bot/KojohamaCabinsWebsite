Ocean Stay Kojohama

Let the coast set the pace.

At Ocean Stay Kojohama, the day begins with the sea.

Morning light reaches the shore before the town fully wakes. The air changes with the tide. Some days are clear and bright; others arrive with wind, cloud, and a horizon that seems to stretch a little farther than before.

Our three independent cabins—Sol, Zen, and Rustic—sit along this quiet edge of Shiraoi. Come for an unhurried night by the water, or stay awhile and use Kojohama as your base for the coast, for Upopoy, and for the small discoveries that appear when there is nowhere else you need to be.

A fishing village, still standing

A place that used to pull its catch ashore by hand.

Until the early Shōwa years, Kojohama's fishermen didn't need a harbor at all—they brought their boats in close to shore, and it was mostly the wives who waded in to haul the nets ashore by hand.

Kojohama · A closer look

More than a stop along the coast.

The street where the cabins now stand was once something bigger. Before Route 36 was built, this stretch—still known as "Fish Street"—was the main road connecting Shiraoi and Noboribetsu, the route everything and everyone passed through.

Many of the families who settled here trace their roots to Niigata, a prefecture known for hard work and quiet endurance—a lineage still visible in a village that has spent generations working this coast.

In more recent years, Kojohama became known for something else entirely: tarako, salted cod roe, grown into a delicacy now sold across Japan.

Good to know, right from the cabins

Ayoro Onsen, open daily from 8am with its own restaurant and lounge. A handful of original tarako shops. A Seicomart for the essentials. And, just down the street, one of the only tarako vending machines in Japan—worth the photo as much as the snack.

Sunrise at the shrine

Walk up to the small shrine for sunrise—best of all on January 1st, but beautiful on any clear morning, especially with the sun rising through the torii gate.

Ayoro Beach & Lighthouse

A short, easy coastal hike to a working lighthouse above the cliffs—one of the best photo spots on this stretch of coast.

The tarako vending machine

One of the very few tarako egg vending machines in all of Japan—a quick stop for a very local souvenir.

Where the boats used to land

Local fishermen still cast lines from this same stretch of sand—the shoreline their grandmothers once waded into, hauling the nets ashore by hand.

The view from the point

From the lighthouse trail, the coastline curves away in both directions—one of the widest, quietest views on this part of the coast.

Cabin 01

Ocean Stay Sol

Open to the light. Close to the sea.

Sol is the cabin for bright mornings and long, easy afternoons. Its coastal-blue details, sunny living space, and sea-view kitchen make it a natural place to linger between walks along the shore.

Make coffee while the light changes over the water. Come back from the beach with sand on your shoes. Let the evening settle in around you.

View Sol on Airbnb

Cabin 02

Ocean Stay Zen

A quieter shade of blue.

Zen is calm without being still. Deep indigo, simple lines, and an open lounge create a space that feels settled from the moment you arrive.

The sea is always nearby—beyond the windows, beyond the terrace, beyond the last conversations of the day. With loft bunks, room to gather, and a displayed samurai armour set, Zen is made for shared time that does not need much planning.

View Zen on Airbnb

Cabin 03

Ocean Stay Rustic

Warmth, texture, and time to settle in.

Rustic carries a different kind of coastal comfort. Brick, wood, tatami, and the gentle familiarity of a kotatsu bring warmth to cooler days by the sea.

There is more to look at here, and more to stay with: vintage character, collected details, and the warm contrast of brick, wood, and tatami. Come in from the weather, make yourself comfortable, and let the outside world slow down for a while.

A little farther from the cabin

A local guide to the coast.

Kojohama is a quiet base for discovering Shiraoi and the southern coast of Hokkaido. Start with the places that help you understand this landscape, then choose a slower afternoon by the water, in the forest, or at the onsen.

Onsen & Stay

Kojohama Onsen Hotel

A long-established hotel known for its high-quality natural hot spring water. A perfect place to experience traditional Japanese hospitality and the healing waters of Kojohama.

Visit the official site → Onsen & Resort

Kokorono Resort Umino Bettei Furukawa

A refined coastal resort focusing on the "spirit of hospitality." Enjoy spectacular ocean views and a peaceful atmosphere designed for complete relaxation.

Visit the official site → Culture

Upopoy National Ainu Museum and Park

A place to learn about Ainu culture, history, language, and living traditions through the museum, park, and daily cultural experiences.

Visit the official site →Nature & making

Nachu no Mori

A gentle garden and natural skincare experience in Kojohama, made for a quiet walk, seasonal scenery, and time to reset.

Visit the official site →Onsen & landscape

Noboribetsu Onsen & Jigokudani

Walk the volcanic landscape of Jigokudani, explore the onsen town, and follow one of Noboribetsu’s official walking courses.
