Kojohama hot-spring guide

Warm mineral water, sea air and an unhurried evening.

Kojohama is one of Hokkaido’s quietly rewarding onsen areas. Several day-bathing options sit close to Ocean Stay, making a hot spring an easy part of a coastal stay—especially from October through March.

Three nearby ways to bathe

Ayoro Onsen

A local, source-fed public bath known for sodium-chloride water. It is a straightforward choice after a coastal walk; check the official page for current hours and closures.

Two kinds of hot-spring water

Shiraoi is an onsen paradise, known for more than 130 hot-spring sources. Two very different types are within easy reach, so pack a bath set and compare them during your stay.

Chloride springs (Kojohama)

The main water around Kojohama, where Ocean Stay sits. Chloride springs keep you warm long after you leave the bath and leave your skin feeling smooth.

Moor springs (towards Lake Poroto)

A short drive towards the Lake Poroto area brings you to moor springs: a brownish, thick water often described as a natural beauty serum for the skin.

Tattoo-friendly private family baths

Public baths in Japan often restrict tattoos, but you can rent a completely private family bath (kashikiri-buro) and enjoy onsen culture with your family or partner in total privacy. It is also a comfortable way for international guests and families with small children to try a hot spring. Two good options are right in the Kojohama area.

Kazokuburo Hama Chidori (家族風呂 浜ちどり)

A Kojohama facility that specialises exclusively in completely private family baths. Its water is 100% free-flowing natural hot spring, which leaves skin feeling silky smooth.

Hana no Yu Onsen (花の湯温泉)

The only hot spring in Shiraoi open 24 hours a day. As well as its standard public baths it offers private family baths, with 100% free-flowing water known for warming the body to the core and smoothing the skin.

Policies, availability, session length and booking arrangements differ by facility and can change, so confirm directly before you go, especially if tattoos are a consideration.

Before entering a Japanese onsen

Wash first

Shower and wash thoroughly before entering the shared bath. Towels stay outside the bathwater.

Check local policies

Tattoo, child, towel-rental and private-bath rules vary by facility. Ask the facility if you are uncertain.

Plan a calm return

Hydrate, allow time to cool down and avoid driving if you feel light-headed. Winter roads require extra time and winter tires.

Hours, fees and temporary closures change. The Shiraoi Tourism Association’s day-trip onsen guide is the best starting point for current choices.
