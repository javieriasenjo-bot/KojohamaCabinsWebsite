Built by Tiny Home

Our three cabins were built by Tiny Home. Yours could be next.

Sol, Zen and Rustic — the three Ocean Stay cabins on the Pacific coast in Kojohama — were built by Tiny Home, a Sapporo company that designs and builds compact cabins for Hokkaido's climate. Stay in one and you are testing the real thing: the insulation in a coastal winter, the morning light, how the space works for a family. If it leaves you picturing a second home, a guest cabin, a small rental or a café of your own, Tiny Home can build it.

Three ways to build small

Container-style Bescon House

Compact container-style units — the type of building you can experience at Ocean Stay.

Bescon Compact Housing

Larger wood-frame compact homes, for living rather than just staying.

Bescon Trailer House

Mobile units that can be moved, for flexible sites and uses.

According to Tiny Home, cold-climate specification comes as standard, including triple-glazed windows. Current models, sizes and prices are listed on their website.

What people build them for

A second home

A small base to return to in every season.

A short-term rental

Guest cabins for minpaku or a small hospitality project.

Staff housing

Comfortable, quick-to-install rooms for seasonal teams.

A café or rest space

Somewhere for visitors to pause — by a farm, a view or a trail.

From order to opening

Tiny Home states that factory production takes about 50–60 days and on-site installation about 5–10 days, and that it can support accommodation projects with property management and bookings. Land, permits and site conditions differ from place to place, so the best first step is a conversation with them.

Try before you build

The surest way to know whether a Tiny Home cabin suits you is to spend a few nights in one. Book Sol, Zen or Rustic, walk the coast, cook in the kitchen, notice how the space feels in the morning — then take what you learned to Tiny Home.
