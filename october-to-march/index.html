October–March

A slower side of Hokkaido, from autumn colour to late-winter light.

October through March brings cooler days, quieter coastlines and more time indoors with the ocean close by. Choose a cabin with the atmosphere and sleeping layout that suits your trip, then check live availability on Airbnb.

October

Cooler coastal walks and autumn travel across southern Hokkaido. Pack layers and leave flexibility for changing weather.

November

A quiet transition month: shorter days, colder air and fewer crowds in many nearby visitor areas.

December–February

Winter conditions require careful transport planning. Confirm forecasts, road information and winter equipment before travel, and allow extra journey time.

March

Late winter begins to soften, but snow, ice and cold conditions can continue. Plan with the same care as mid-winter.

Ski, then come home

Two of the world’s great powder resorts — and your own cabin at the end of the day

Rusutsu is about 70 minutes from the cabins. Niseko is about 90. That is close enough to catch the first lift, ski Hokkaido powder all day, and still be back on this coast before dinner — to a whole cabin that is yours alone, with the northern sea in the window, instead of a corridor in a resort hotel.

It also means you are not paying resort-village rates to sleep. Drive out after breakfast, drive back in the afternoon, run a hot bath, cook in your own kitchen, and watch the light go off the water.

Both drives follow main roads, but they are winter roads. Snow tyres are standard on Hokkaido rental cars; allow more time than the map suggests. Read the winter driving guide →
